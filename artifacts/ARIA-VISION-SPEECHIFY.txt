ARIA SITE VISION. For Nick Williams. To listen. Not a build plan. Not a how-to. This is what I want the site to be, how I want it to look, how I want it to act, and what I want it to do for a person who lands on it. I will say things I am not sure we can make. That is the point. You asked for the vision, as long as it will run.

This is not a summer evening story. This is the product I can see.

Chapter one. What the first ten seconds feel like.

You open the site and you are not in a portal. You are not in a grid of houses fighting for your eye. You are in Aiken. The first thing is not a search bar the size of a billboard. The first thing is a place. The camera is already in the air, not spinning, not showing off, just held. You see canopy. You see the pale sand roads. You see the way the live oaks hold the light. You see downtown as a small true thing, not a stock photo of a generic Main Street. The brand navy sits still. The gold is a thin line, not a trophy. Your name is there, Nick Williams, Coldwell Banker Best Life Realty, the way a person is named on a door, not the way a lead form is named on a landing page.

There is almost nothing to click yet and that is on purpose. The site is not hungry. It does not throw a chat at your face. It does not say Contact an agent. It does not say I found three thousand homes. It lets the place arrive.

Then the map is the room. Not a tiny map in a corner. The map is the floor. Listings are quiet. Pins do not scream. They wait. When you move, the world moves with you, the way a good car’s hood stays still while the road changes. Filters exist, but they behave like asking a person in the passenger seat, not like filling out a loan application. Beds. Price. A place name. Hitchcock Woods. Downtown. North Augusta. Graniteville. You can speak it or you can type it or you can just drag the map with your thumb.

Rou is there, but she is not a bubble in the corner begging. She is a presence the way a good host is a presence. If the talking-head ever exists, she is a person on the picture, head moving, voice Carina, captions on the picture like television, not cream boxes of text stacked like a chatbot from 2019. If the talking-head is not ready, she is still not a cartoon orb. She is a voice and a name and a quiet face that does not pretend to be you, Gholi, or a stock model. She does not sell. She orients.

The first ten seconds should make a person from anywhere in the world think, this is a specific town, this is a specific human, this is not Zillow with a different logo.

Chapter two. The map as a living place.

I want the map to have weather that belongs to this parcel, not a widget from a phone. If it is raining on Whiskey Road it is raining on the map. If the light is late and long, the map knows it is late. Clouds can exist. The sky can exist. I do not know if we can do that well. I want it anyway. I want a person in Ohio to feel the humidity of the Sandhills without us writing the word humidity.

I want the canopy to be the vastness. Not a sphere. Not a fake cathedral. Aiken’s trees are the cathedral. When you fly the camera toward Hitchcock Woods I want the body to do that small thing it does when scale is real. Reduced motion still gets the stills of that vastness. We do not punish the people who cannot take a moving camera. The still photograph of the woods at the right hour should be enough to hush a room.

I want horse land to look like horse land. Paddock is not lawn. A dry lot is not a park. If a listing has a barn I want the map to stop treating that acre as a suburban yard with a mower. Polo is not a lifestyle badge. It is land use. Golf is not a filter that ranks people. It is a course on the ground with a name.

I want the map to tell the truth about distance without ranking neighborhoods. How far to the hospital. How far to Savannah River Site as a drive time, dated, from a public page, not from a rumor. How far to Augusta. How far to downtown. A line on the map. Walk or drive. No sentence that says this is a good area. No sentence that says this is a bad area. The land is the land.

I want lot lines when we have a real survey or a real county polygon, and I want the site to refuse to draw a pretty fence as a property line when we do not. Three layers, always, in how it acts: the survey if it exists, the county footprint if it exists, the fence as a thing a person built. The site says which one you are looking at. It never lets a Microsoft building outline pretend to be a deed.

I want you to be able to stand the map up in satellite and streets without feeling like you entered a different product. Same navy. Same quiet. The house is still the house.

Chapter three. Rou. What she does. How she acts. How she looks.

Rou is Node A. Public. Short memory. She is the utility of the place. She flies the camera. She hears “show me porches” and the map answers. She hears “how far to Bridgestone” and she draws a route. She hears “I like this one” and she does not throw a form. She marks it. She is allowed to be warm. She is not allowed to be a girlfriend, a therapist, or a closer.

I want her voice to be Carina only. I want her captions on the picture, broadcast style, so a person who cannot hear still gets the sentence, and a person who muted TikTok-style still gets the sentence. Mute is a first-class act. The site never shames mute. Half the world watches without sound.

I want her to refuse with grace. If someone asks which neighborhood has the good schools, she does not play along and she does not lecture like a compliance robot. She says we do not rank people or schools here, and here is Nick, and here is the contact path. If someone asks a Fair Housing thing, the pipe catches it before she performs an opinion. The human handoff is always in the room. Talk to Nick. The contact page. The phone in hours. After hours, a path that is a path, not a dead end.

I want her to know the listing you have open. She does not invent square feet. She does not invent a pool. She says what the MLS gave us and she says what is missing. Missing is a feature. Missing looks like a quiet card that says we do not have stories on this one. We do not have living area from this feed. We will not guess.

I want her to schedule a showing by stating that Nick will submit it, not by asking if you want to speak to an agent. Guests get a simple path. Signed-in people get one tap. She never pretends she is Nick.

I want her face, when it is real, to be a talking head that talks. The head moves. It is not a still of a lounge. It is not Gholi’s dashboard face. It is not the workbench photograph. Until that exists, the site does not fake it with a prettier stock woman.

I want Rou to be able to sit in a rail beside the map, not as a takeover. The house is the star. She is the instrument.

Chapter four. One house. The listing as a place for truth.

When you open a home I want the page to feel like you walked up the steps, not like you opened a product detail page for a blender.

The photographs are large. They are not a strip of thumbnails that lie. If a photo is virtually staged, I want the original empty room next to it, always, like a before and after that is honest instead of salesy. A small mark that says this one is digitally altered. Furniture only. No fake windows. No fake flooring. If we cannot pair them, we do not run the staged one alone and hope.

I want noon. I want the site to be able to show this kitchen at a real hour against the twilight listing shot. I want a person to feel the difference in their body. The HDR glow is a known trick. Our site does not scold the other photographer. It just lets daylight in.

I want a floor plan that came from a walk through the room with a phone that can see depth, not from a single pretty JPEG hallucinated into walls. If we do not have that walk, the plan is absent, and absence looks calm, not like a broken image. If we have a Gaussian splat or a dollhouse from a real scan, it is the scan of this house. You can move. You can look up at the porch ceiling. You cannot paint a wall that does not exist and call it the house.

Facts sit in a language a person can hear. Price we have seen, not a fake history from another website. Listing courtesy of the office that actually listed it, never defaulting to your shop. Beds, baths, what the feed truly has. Roof as a word the MLS gave, not a novel. Heating as a type, not a SEER we invented. A missing-fact list that is visible. People should leave that page knowing what we do not know.

I want a taught seller disclosure, when we have it, that is not a PDF dumped at the bottom. It is a map of the house in yes, no, and the seller did not represent. Water. Sewer. Roof. Foundation. Termites. Lead. Radon. Tanks. Each one a room. ARIA does not answer for the seller. The inspector is a later document. We show that there are three truths and they can disagree.

I want county records as a door, not as scraped gossip. A link. A date. We did not launder the assessor into our voice.

I want compare from here. Add this home. Up to a few. Not a ranking. A table of facts. You will feel two houses beside each other the way you feel two trucks in a lot. Price. Beds. What is missing on each. No winner badge. No ninety-nine percent match to your soul.

I want save to be one tap. Yes and no on a house for the notebook. Trash and restore. No extra consent theater. The public site does not become a guilt machine.

Request a showing is one clear act. It looks like a door, not a funnel.

Chapter five. The photograph as an object.

I am almost more interested in the photograph than in the chat.

I want every picture to have a life. When it was made. Whether it was edited. Whether the camera wrote a content credential. Whether we still have the original. A listing photo should act like a museum object, not like a story on a phone that disappears.

If the photographer can keep credentials in the file, the site can show a small true mark: this came from a camera, this was cropped, this was staged. If the credentials were stripped, we do not pretend.

I want reverse-honesty more than reverse-image search. If this photo has been used on another house, that is a scandal we do not invent, but I want the culture of the site to make reuse feel impossible because originals live here.

I want you, on a showing, to be able to stand in the room and take the noon shot that will sit beside the listing twilight. That pair is a feature. It looks like two stills. It acts like a lie detector that does not call anyone a liar.

Chapter six. Compare, but not the way portals compare.

I want compare to grow up.

Two houses, yes. Also two futures. This house versus waiting a year, with the mortgage rate dated from a public source, not a prediction of prices. Rent versus buy, with every assumption written on the card, no scare. Cash versus a United States Department of Agriculture well test versus a V A clock versus a conventional clock. Time is a fact. Loan type is not a moral type of person.

I want list price versus what it would cost to rebuild, when we have a serious number, and I want us to refuse a fake insurance premium. The card says these are different dollars.

I want seller concession on the MLS versus what a buyer broker might be paid off the MLS. Two different dollars. The site never says the seller pays everything and never says commissions are a law of nature. Fees are negotiable. That sentence is visible at the showing. It is not a pop-up on first visit.

I want owned solar versus leased solar. A lease that rides the house should look like a red honest card, sourced from title, not from a listing blurb that says solar as if it were a gift.

I want city water versus well. Sewer versus septic. One hundred amp versus two hundred. A termite bond that transfers versus a bond that dies. These comparisons are how a grown person buys a house in South Carolina. They should look as considered as price.

I want one acre of lawn versus one acre of paddock versus timber versus wet ground. The mower clock does not run on a paddock. The site acts like it knows the difference in its bones.

Chapter seven. The lifetime of the house. Energy, water, roof, the bill.

I want a room called the next ten years, and a room called the length of a mortgage, and I want them to look like arithmetic a person can distrust in a healthy way. Every number has a source and a date. If we do not have the source, the room is empty and says so.

I want the nameplate. I want a photograph of the outdoor unit, the water heater, the panel, the inverter. I want the model to mean something. If the Air Conditioning, Heating, and Refrigeration Institute directory still knows that model, we show the certified rating they published. We do not invent a S E E R from the word heat pump. If the unit is old and the directory forgot it, we say the directory does not have it. Age from a serial, if the serial tells the truth, as a year, not as a scare.

I want the yellow EnergyGuide sticker, if it is still on the appliance, to be a source. A photo. Not a guess.

I want tankless versus tank to use real water-heater math, not a blog. I want us to be allowed to say we used national average use as a stand-in, and I want that stand-in to look slightly ashamed, because it is a stand-in. Aiken is not a national average. If we ever have a bill, the bill wins.

I want siding and roof to refuse a stupid conversion. Vinyl is not a kilowatt-hour. Architectural shingles are not automatically a cool roof. Reflectance is reflectance. A metal roof in climate zone three A is a story about sun and also about heat you might want in January. I want the site to hold two true things without becoming a salesman for a product.

I want ten-year cost sitting next to mortgage-length cost, with the method named. Present value if we use it. Inflation if we use it. A person should be able to disagree with the method and still respect the card.

I want paint as light. Light reflectance value as a number, if we have the paint, not as a mood board.

I want gas versus electric as a rates card from the utility, dated, not as a culture war. Induction as a panel question. Can this panel take it. Absence if we never opened the door.

I want solar only with inputs. Size, tilt, azimuth, or we label the whole thing a hypothetical in big honest type. Net metering as the utility actually writes it this year, not as a California memory.

I want the site to act like a place that would rather be quiet than viral about a fake savings number.

I want rain on this parcel from the weather service, not from a vibe. Alerts when the government has alerts. No climate letter grade we invented. No borrowed score from a company that sells fear as a ten.
